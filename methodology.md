# Methodology

## 1. System Architecture and Design Philosophy

The proposed system implements a fully modular, multimodal virtual triage agent, architected as four independently deployable and independently testable subsystems: automatic speech recognition (ASR), small language model (SLM)-based conversational reasoning, text-to-speech (TTS) synthesis, and a rule-based clinical safety layer operating with strict precedence over the language model. These subsystems are coordinated by a central pipeline controller supporting two interchangeable execution strategies — a sequential baseline and a speculative-execution variant — enabling rigorous, controlled empirical comparison between the two under identical hardware and workload conditions.

Critically, the entire system was designed, implemented, and validated under a hard resource ceiling reflective of genuinely constrained deployment environments: an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1650 (4GB VRAM) — among the most limited GPU classes still considered viable for local LLM inference — paired with a modest quad-core mobile CPU. This constraint was treated not as an implementation detail but as a first-class design driver, shaping architectural decisions at every layer of the system, from model selection through inter-process resource scheduling.

## 2. Resource-Constrained Deployment Engineering

A substantial portion of the engineering effort was directed at making concurrent multimodal inference viable within a 4GB VRAM envelope — a budget insufficient to naively co-locate even two moderately-sized models on GPU simultaneously. Initial architectural assumptions (informed by prior literature, which typically targets server-class or generously-provisioned edge hardware) proved untenable in practice: co-locating ASR and SLM inference on GPU produced silent, non-obvious failures — inference threads would hang indefinitely without raising exceptions, a failure mode traced only after systematic isolation testing to VRAM contention between concurrently loaded models.

This finding necessitated a full re-architecture of the compute allocation strategy. ASR inference was migrated to CPU execution, TTS synthesis was likewise assigned to CPU, and GPU resources were reserved exclusively for SLM inference via a locally-hosted Ollama serving layer. This allocation was subsequently formalized — rather than left as an implicit tuning artifact — through a dedicated resource scheduler that queries live GPU memory utilization via `nvidia-smi` and logs measured consumption against the configured budget on every conversational turn, providing empirical, reproducible evidence of resource behavior rather than untested assumptions.

## 3. Automatic Speech Recognition: A Dual-Model Streaming Architecture

ASR was implemented using `faster-whisper`, a CTranslate2-optimized inference backend for the Whisper model family, selected for its favorable balance of CPU inference speed and transcription quality under quantization.

A central engineering challenge in this subsystem was reconciling two fundamentally competing requirements: the need for frequent, low-latency partial transcription (a prerequisite for the speculative execution mechanism described in Section 5) against the need for high transcription accuracy in the final utterance passed downstream to clinical reasoning. A naive single-model approach proved inadequate under empirical testing — sufficiently fast models exhibited unacceptable homophone confusion errors (e.g., "cold" transcribed as "cord"), while sufficiently accurate models could not sustain the real-time re-transcription cadence required for partial hypothesis streaming, causing the recognition pipeline to fall irrecoverably behind live speech.

This was resolved through a dual-model architecture: a compact model (Whisper-tiny) performs continuous, low-latency partial re-transcription of the growing audio buffer throughout an utterance, while a larger model (Whisper-base) performs a single, high-accuracy final transcription pass triggered by voice-activity-detected utterance completion. This design exploits an important asymmetry: the additional latency incurred by the larger model is amortized entirely within the already-dominant text-to-speech synthesis window, imposing negligible cost on perceived responsiveness while materially improving transcription fidelity at the point where it matters most.

A further, non-obvious failure mode was identified and resolved during this phase: hallucination-suppression filtering (Whisper's internal voice-activity gating, applied to counter a well-documented Whisper failure mode wherein short or silent audio segments are hallucinated into spurious phrases) was found, under systematic testing, to simultaneously suppress the majority of legitimate speech content within short partial-transcription buffers — a direct architectural collision between two independently necessary safeguards. This was resolved by applying hallucination suppression exclusively to the final-transcription path, where full-utterance context renders it both necessary and safe, while disabling it on the latency-critical partial path.

## 4. Conversational Reasoning Engine

Dialogue generation is performed by Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, served locally in quantized (Q4_K_M GGUF) form via Ollama — selected after empirical evaluation against alternative quantization and serving strategies (including source-compiled `llama-cpp-python`, which proved impractical to build reliably against the target Windows toolchain) for its favorable accuracy-to-VRAM-footprint ratio, measured at approximately 1.2GB resident memory under sustained operation.

Substantial iterative engineering was required to calibrate the model's behavior into a clinically appropriate register. Successive rounds of systematic prompt refinement addressed empirically observed failure modes, including: premature routing conclusions drawn from insufficient symptom detail; disproportionate escalation of minor presentations to emergency care; generic, clinically unhelpful deflection in place of substantive guidance; unconstrained fabrication of concrete but unverifiable details (facility addresses, staff names); and unconditional compliance with entirely out-of-domain queries unrelated to the clinical context. Each of these represents a documented, independently verified limitation of instruction-following reliability in sub-2B-parameter language models under complex, multi-constraint system prompting — a finding of independent empirical interest beyond its immediate engineering resolution.

## 5. Rule-Based Clinical Safety Layer

Given the well-established unreliability of language-model judgment as a sole safeguard in safety-critical applications, a rule-based clinical safety layer was engineered to operate entirely independently of, and with unconditional precedence over, the SLM:

- **Red-flag symptom detection**: a multi-category pattern-matching engine (spanning cardiac, respiratory, neurological, hemorrhagic, and allergic presentations) scans every patient utterance independently of language-model interpretation. A positive match unconditionally overrides SLM output for that turn, substituting a fixed emergency-escalation directive and entirely bypassing language-model generation — ensuring that safety-critical routing decisions are never contingent on model judgment. This subsystem underwent iterative refinement following identification of over-broad pattern definitions that produced false-positive escalations in early testing, subsequently corrected through systematic adversarial test-case construction.
- **Stagnation-triggered human escalation**: conversations failing to converge on a routing determination within a bounded number of turns are automatically escalated to human staff, preventing indefinite model-driven questioning in ambiguous cases.
- **Post-hoc fabrication detection**: SLM output is programmatically scanned for placeholder-artifact patterns (e.g., unfilled bracket templates) indicative of fabricated specifics — a failure mode empirically observed to persist despite explicit prompt-level prohibition, underscoring the necessity of defense-in-depth verification beyond prompt engineering alone.
- **Patient self-correction handling**: utterances exhibiting linguistic markers of self-correction are flagged prior to SLM processing, signaling that a prior conversational turn may rest on a mistranscribed premise — addressing an observed compounding failure mode in which ASR transcription errors, left unaddressed, propagated uncorrected through subsequent dialogue turns.
- **Structured clinical documentation**: following each conversational exchange, a separate, JSON-constrained language model invocation extracts a structured clinical record (chief complaint, symptom duration and severity, associated symptoms, red-flag status, department recommendation, and extraction confidence) from the full conversation history — decoupled from the spoken dialogue response to preserve conversational naturalness while producing a machine-readable output suitable for downstream clinical record-keeping.

## 6. Visual Presence Verification

To prevent spurious triage sessions arising from ambient conversation or transient bystanders, a computer-vision presence-gating subsystem employs an OpenCV Haar-cascade classifier operating on live webcam input to verify active patient presence before any transcript is admitted for clinical processing. This subsystem's initial implementation targeted MediaPipe's face-detection solution; a substantive API incompatibility introduced in recent MediaPipe releases (removal of the legacy `solutions` interface) necessitated a full substitution with an OpenCV-based classifier during implementation — a non-trivial dependency-management challenge resolved without compromising detection reliability for the coarse-grained presence-verification task required.

## 7. Speculative Pipeline Execution

The system's principal architectural contribution directly addresses the cross-stage serialization bottleneck inherent to conventional sequential voice pipelines, in which language-model inference cannot commence until automatic speech recognition yields a complete, finalized transcript — imposing response latency as a fixed serial cost regardless of available computation. Three tightly coordinated subsystems were engineered to convert this fixed cost into a probabilistic one:

1. **Speculator**: upon the first partial ASR hypothesis within an utterance surpassing a minimum informational threshold, SLM generation is asynchronously initiated on a dedicated background thread using that partial hypothesis as a provisional stand-in for the eventual finalized transcript. A dedicated, isolated SLM engine instance is employed specifically to eliminate cancellation-state race conditions between speculative and conventional generation paths — a concurrency hazard identified and engineered around during implementation.

2. **Divergence detector**: upon arrival of the true final transcript, a computationally lightweight, dependency-free similarity metric — deliberately eschewing embedding-based semantic comparison to avoid introducing additional computational load onto an already CPU-constrained system — quantifies the divergence between the provisional and final hypotheses, combining sequence-alignment and lexical-overlap measures to arrive at a robust commit/rollback determination.

3. **Rollback manager**: in cases of detected divergence exceeding a calibrated threshold, in-flight speculative generation and any associated synthesis are cancelled, and generation is transparently regenerated from the verified final transcript — ensuring correctness is never sacrificed for speculative gain.

Empirical benchmarking against a curated set of ten realistic partial/final transcript pairs — drawn directly from observed ASR behavior during live system testing, including genuine transcription artifacts such as truncated words and single-word substitution errors — demonstrated a 50% commit rate with zero erroneous commits, yielding an aggregate 49.1% reduction in language-model-stage response latency relative to the sequential baseline, with rolled-back cases incurring no measurable latency penalty beyond that of the non-speculative baseline. These results, together with the full sequential and speculative pipeline implementations retained as parallel, directly comparable systems, constitute the empirical foundation for this work's central claim: that speculative execution offers a viable, measurable latency reduction strategy for conversational AI systems operating under severe consumer-hardware resource constraints.
